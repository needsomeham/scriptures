**In one line:** The main live methodological fault line in NT textual criticism: should the text with the most surviving manuscript copies (Byzantine) be preferred by default, or should editors weigh internal and external evidence case-by-case (reasoned eclecticism) even when that favors a minority of (often older) witnesses?
**What it is:** **Byzantine priority**, argued most systematically by Maurice A. Robinson (Southeastern Baptist Theological Seminary) with William G. Pierpont, holds that normal scribal transmission dynamics mean the reading preserved in the majority of manuscripts is statistically more likely to be original, since a genuinely original reading should propagate and dominate over time absent strong countervailing corruption pressure [T-18][T-05]. **Reasoned eclecticism** (NA/UBS/ECM's method, see [[tc-reasoned-eclecticism]]) replies that surviving manuscript counts reflect *when and where* copying was heaviest (the Byzantine text dominates precisely because it was the standard text of the Byzantine Empire's later, high-volume copying era), not textual priority — a few early papyri or Alexandrian majuscules can outweigh a numerical majority of centuries-later copies [T-10]. Related but distinct: the confessional/theological "KJV-only" or "Textus Receptus-only" movement, which argues for the Byzantine-derived Textus Receptus on doctrinal/providential grounds rather than Robinson's transmission-probability argument — the two should not be conflated, as Robinson explicitly frames his case as text-critical, not theological.
**Why it matters for reading the NT:** Nearly every disputed passage in [[tc-major-disputed-passages]] (Mark 16:9-20, Comma Johanneum, etc.) is exactly where these two methods diverge most visibly — understanding the debate explains why some Bible translations/footnotes differ from others on these verses.
**State of scholarship:** Reasoned eclecticism (NA/UBS/ECM) is the `majority view`/institutional consensus in academic textual criticism; Byzantine priority is a `minority view` held by a small but serious group of credentialed scholars (Robinson, Pierpont, and some at Byzantine-priority-sympathetic institutions); KJV-only/TR-only positions are a further `minority view`, generally not engaged with as text-critical scholarship by the mainstream academy since their premises are confessional rather than evidentiary.
**Where it breaks / cautions:** Avoid strawmanning either side — Robinson's argument is a genuine statistical/transmissional claim answerable on its own terms (and [[tc-cbgm]] research is, in part, a more rigorous way of testing exactly this kind of transmission-pattern claim), not simply "manuscript counting."
