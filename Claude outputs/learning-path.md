# Greek learning path (for Jacob)

Reading-first, video/course-driven, free-first. Built only from notes that
exist in `sources/`. Each stage interleaves a Greek track with parallel
"context" reading (background + textual criticism) — don't do Greek in a
vacuum. See `[[moc-greek-learning]]`, `[[moc-nt-background]]`,
`[[moc-textual-criticism]]` for full domain listings.

## Weekly loop (from Stage 1 onward)
- **5x 20–30 min reading** — [[daily-dose-of-greek]] + whatever reader/text
  the stage specifies.
- **2x grammar** — a lesson/chapter of the stage's spine grammar + drills.
- **1x lecture/context** — one background or TC video/lecture (rotates
  through the parallel track below).
Adjust ratio down (not out) when busy: keep the daily 10–15 min Greek
touch even on a skipped grammar week — consistency beats intensity.

## Stage 0 — Alphabet, pronunciation, tooling (~2 weeks)
**Goal:** read the alphabet fluently, pick a pronunciation, set up tools.
- Learn the alphabet + diacritics (breathing, accents, iota subscript) via
  the intro chapters of [[mounce-basics-of-biblical-greek]] or
  [[croy-primer-of-biblical-greek]] (library copy is fine at this stage).
- Decide a pronunciation scheme using
  [[pronunciation-erasmian-vs-restored-koine]] (neutral overview of
  Erasmian/restored-Koine/Modern). Recommendation for a reading-first,
  non-liturgical learner: **Erasmian** (matches nearly all free video
  courses below) unless you plan to pursue [[biblical-language-center-living-koine-greek]]-style immersion, which uses restored Koine.
- Set up tools: [[step-bible]] (interlinear/morphology), [[logeion]]
  (lexicon lookups), [[daily-dose-of-greek]] (start the daily habit now,
  even before you understand most of it — ear-training).
- Skim [[billmounce-site]] for free vocab-flashcard tooling.
**Ready for Stage 1 when:** you can name/write all 24 letters and
diacritics from memory and have STEP Bible + Logeion bookmarked.

## Stage 1 — First-year grammar spine (~4–6 months)
**Goal:** morphology + core syntax, reading-first.
- **Spine grammar (primary): [[decker-reading-koine-greek]].** Recommended
  over Mounce as the spine because it is reading-first from lesson 1 (real
  NT sentences, not invented drill sentences), teaches verbs aspect-first
  (aspect-primacy is the `majority view` in current Greek linguistics —
  `contested` in degree, see [[decker-reading-koine-greek]]),
  and integrates a workbook. Trade-off: less video support than Mounce, so
  pair it with the video track below.
- **Video track: [[biblicaltraining-mounce-nt201]]** (free, full course) —
  use for the lecture explanations even while working through Decker's
  reading passages; Mounce's paradigm drilling complements Decker's
  reading orientation. [[billmounce-site]] for supplementary vocab
  flashcards and "Monday with Mounce" review videos.
- **Alternative/backup spine:** [[mounce-basics-of-biblical-greek]] or
  [[croy-primer-of-biblical-greek]] if Decker's pace feels too fast, or
  free [[funk-beginning-intermediate-grammar]] if cost is the constraint.
- Keep the [[daily-dose-of-greek]] habit running throughout — it will feel
  ahead of your grammar for months; that's fine, it's ear/eye training.
- **Parallel context track:** watch [[bg-yale-dale-martin-lectures]] (Open
  Yale, free, full course) — one lecture per week or two, no rush. This
  gives the historical-critical frame while your Greek is still basic.
- Note (scope caveat): if tempted by [[athenaze-attic-caveat]] or other
  "reading-first classical Greek" primers — those teach **Attic**, not
  Koine; skip them for this path.
**Ready for Stage 2 when:** you can parse any regular verb/noun form,
recognize the moods/voices, and read a simplified/glossed NT sentence
without stopping every word.

## Stage 2 — Reading in earnest (~6–12 months)
**Goal:** sustained reading of real NT Greek, references introduced.
- **Reading order:** 1 John → Mark → John → shorter Pauline letters
  (Philippians, 1 Thessalonians). 1 John first for its simple vocabulary
  and repetition; Mark for narrative pace; John to consolidate; Pauline
  letters to meet denser argumentative syntax.
- **Reader's edition (pick one, use daily):**
  [[tyndale-house-greek-nt-readers-edition]] or [[ubs5-readers-edition]]
  — footnote-glossed rare words so you read continuously instead of
  constantly dictionary-diving.
- Keep [[daily-dose-of-greek]] running; add [[step-bible]] for anything
  the reader's footnotes don't cover.
- **Introduce references:** [[bdag]] (the lexicon of record — worth
  buying by now), [[wallace-greek-grammar-beyond-basics]] (syntax
  questions as they come up — don't read it cover to cover),
  [[runge-discourse-grammar]] (why authors chose that word order/
  connective — read alongside John's discourse-heavy prose).
  [[louw-nida-lexicon]] for semantic-domain word studies;
  [[logeion]]/[[abbott-smith-lexicon]] as free lexicon options if BDAG
  isn't affordable yet.
- **Parallel background track:** [[bg-ehrman-new-testament-historical-introduction]]
  or [[bg-powell-introducing-the-new-testament]] as your intro-textbook
  spine; [[bg-keener-ivp-bible-background-commentary]] as a verse-by-verse
  companion while reading Mark/John; [[bg-desilva-honor-patronage-kinship-purity]]
  for the honor-shame/purity social world (see [[honor-shame-culture]]);
  [[second-temple-judaism]] and [[septuagint-and-the-nt]] concept notes.
- **Parallel TC track:** [[tc-metzger-ehrman-text-of-the-new-testament]]
  (the standard survey — read this now, before the apparatus work of
  Stage 3) and, for a critical popular counterpoint on both over- and
  under-claiming, [[tc-ehrman-misquoting-jesus]] alongside
  [[tc-hixson-gurry-myths-and-mistakes]]. Start noticing footnote markers
  in your reader's edition — that's the on-ramp to Stage 3.
**Ready for Stage 3 when:** you can read a chapter of 1 John or Mark with
the reader's edition at conversational pace (occasional lookups, not
constant), and you understand what a footnote/apparatus marker is
pointing at even if you can't yet resolve it yourself.

## Stage 3 — Intermediate: apparatus, discourse, LXX (~ongoing, no fixed end)
**Goal:** read with the apparatus, not just the running text; broaden into
aspect/discourse theory and the LXX.
- **Apparatus reading:** work through [[tc-reading-a-critical-apparatus]]
  (concept note) alongside [[tc-metzger-textual-commentary-gnt]] — pick a
  variant from your current reading and look up *why* the committee
  decided as it did. Use [[tc-na28-novum-testamentum-graece]] or
  [[tc-sblgnt]] (free) as your working critical text.
- **Aspect & discourse:** revisit [[runge-discourse-grammar]] more
  systematically; explore the aspect debate via [[decker-reading-koine-greek]]'s
  framing and [[wallace-greek-grammar-beyond-basics]] for the mainstream
  reference position — note this is a live scholarly debate (Porter vs.
  Fanning vs. Campbell), not settled.
- **LXX sampling:** start reading short LXX passages (e.g. Psalms, Jonah)
  alongside [[bg-jobes-silva-invitation-septuagint]] and the free
  full-text [[bg-nets-septuagint-online]]; cross-reference NT quotations
  of the OT via [[bg-beale-carson-nt-use-of-ot]] and the
  [[septuagint-and-the-nt]] concept note.
- **Parallel TC track — method:** [[tc-cbgm]] and
  [[tc-wasserman-gurry-cbgm]] (paid, no free equivalent of the same
  depth — see gap ledger) for the field's current genealogical method;
  [[tc-byzantine-priority-debate]] and [[tc-reasoned-eclecticism]] for
  the live methodological fault line; [[tc-major-disputed-passages]] as
  a checklist to work through (Mark 16, John 7:53–8:11, 1 John 5:7).
- **Parallel background track:** deepen with [[bg-sanders-judaism-practice-belief]],
  [[bg-charlesworth-ot-pseudepigrapha]] or [[bg-vermes-dead-sea-scrolls-english]]
  for [[jewish-apocalyptic]]/[[second-temple-judaism]], and
  [[gospel-genre-greco-roman-biography]]/[[authorship-and-dating-overview]]
  for the literary-critical questions behind the Gospels you're reading.
**Signs you've arrived:** you read a UBS5/NA28 footnote and know what
question it's answering before consulting Metzger; you can explain why a
translation committee chose a reading, in claim-ladder terms.

## Free-only path (variant)
Swap every paid item for its free sibling; nothing below costs money:
Stage 0 unchanged (all free). Stage 1: [[funk-beginning-intermediate-grammar]]
as spine (or a library copy of Mounce/Decker) + [[biblicaltraining-mounce-nt201]]
+ [[billmounce-site]] + [[daily-dose-of-greek]]. Stage 2: [[abbott-smith-lexicon]]
and [[logeion]] instead of BDAG; [[tc-sblgnt]] or the free
[[tc-tyndale-house-greek-new-testament]] online text instead of a print
reader's edition; [[bg-yale-dale-martin-lectures]] + [[bg-nt-pod-goodacre]]
+ library copies of intro textbooks. Stage 3: [[tc-cbgm]] (concept note,
free) instead of Wasserman & Gurry's book; [[bg-nets-septuagint-online]]
for LXX text.

## Paid items worth it (when budget allows)
1. **[[decker-reading-koine-greek]]** (~$50–60) — the reading-first spine;
   biggest pedagogical upgrade over free alternatives.
2. **[[bdag]]** (~$90–150) — no free lexicon matches its depth; buy once
   you're reading real text in Stage 2.
3. **[[tyndale-house-greek-nt-readers-edition]]** (~$40–50) — makes
   sustained reading dramatically less friction-filled.
4. **[[wallace-greek-grammar-beyond-basics]]** (~$50–55) — the standard
   syntax reference once you're asking "why this case/mood" questions.
5. **[[runge-discourse-grammar]]** (~$35–45) — the best single upgrade for
   understanding *why* an author wrote a sentence the way he did.
