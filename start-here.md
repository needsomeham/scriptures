# Start here (Stage 0 kickoff for Jacob)

You know the English NT well and think in engineering terms. Good — Greek
here rewards that: pattern recognition over memorization, reading over
flashcards. This page is the concrete on-ramp; [[learning-path]] has the
full curriculum. Everything below is free.

## (a) Decide once: pronunciation
See [[pronunciation-erasmian-vs-restored-koine]] (https://danielstreett.com/2011/12/01/the-great-greek-pronunciation-debate-sbl-2011-report-pt-3/) for the full neutral overview. Short version:
- **Erasmian** — 16th-c. reconstruction, historically inaccurate but it's
  what nearly every seminary, textbook, and free video course uses.
- **Restored Koine** — linguistically closer to how 1st-century speakers
  actually sounded (majority view among historical phonologists), used
  by immersion programs like [[biblical-language-center-living-koine-greek]].
- Neither affects grammar or comprehension — it's purely how you voice
  the letters.
- Trade-off: restored Koine is the historically "truer" sound; Erasmian
  is the practically compatible one.
- **Default: Erasmian**, because [[daily-dose-of-greek]] and
  [[biblicaltraining-mounce-nt201]] (your Weeks 1–2 tools below) both use
  it. Switching later costs you a few weeks of re-hearing sounds, not
  months — it is cheap to change once you have reasons to.

## (c) Weeks 1–2, day by day (10–25 min/day)
1. **Day 1 (20 min):** Alphabet chunk 1 — α β γ δ ε ζ η θ (names + sounds,
   Erasmian). Use [[mounce-basics-of-biblical-greek]] ch. 1 or
   [[greek-alphabet-and-diacritics]] as your reference table.
2. **Day 2 (20 min):** Alphabet chunk 2 — ι κ λ μ ν ξ ο π. Say each aloud.
3. **Day 3 (20 min):** Alphabet chunk 3 — ρ σ/ς τ υ φ χ ψ ω. Full alphabet
   review, uppercase + lowercase + final sigma.
4. **Day 4 (15 min):** Breathings (smooth/rough) and the iota subscript —
   see [[greek-alphabet-and-diacritics]]. Practice on 5 real NT words.
5. **Day 5 (15 min):** The three accents (acute/grave/circumflex) — what
   they mark, why you can mostly ignore them for now (pitch, not stress,
   in the historical system). Read [[greek-alphabet-and-diacritics]].
6. **Day 6 (15 min):** Read the whole alphabet aloud, uppercase and
   lowercase, from memory. Time yourself; repeat until under 60 seconds.
7. **Day 7 (10 min):** First [[daily-dose-of-greek]] video — pick any
   verse from the archive, just get the habit started.
   (https://dailydoseofgreek.com/)
8. **Day 8 (25 min):** [[biblicaltraining-mounce-nt201]] Lecture 1 — "The
   Greek Language" (alphabet, pronunciation, grammar overview).
9. **Day 9 (25 min):** NT201 Lecture 3 — "The Alphabet and Pronunciation"
   (gamma nasals, vowels, diphthongs, breathing marks in more depth).
10. **Day 10 (20 min):** Transcribe 1 John 1:1 letter-by-letter by hand
    from a printed/screen Greek text (e.g. via [[step-bible]] or
    [[tc-sblgnt]]) — slow, deliberate, no typing.
11. **Day 11 (20 min):** Second [[daily-dose-of-greek]] video; note any
    letters/sounds still shaky and drill those specifically.
12. **Day 12 (25 min):** Open **[[step-bible]]**
    (https://www.stepbible.org/) and hover-parse every word of 1 John
    1:1–4 — just look, don't try to memorize the grammar terms yet.
13. **Day 13 (15 min):** NT201 Lecture 4 — "Punctuation and
    Syllabification." Re-read your Day 10 transcription and check it
    against the syllable rules.
14. **Day 14 (15 min):** Read 1 John 1:1–4 aloud, Erasmian pronunciation,
    from the text you transcribed. Don't worry about comprehension —
    this is decoding fluency. This is the same passage
    [[1-john-1-1-4-first-reading]] walks through in more depth.
