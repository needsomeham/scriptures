**In one line:** The single best one-volume survey of the whole Greco-Roman and Jewish world the NT was written into.
**What it is:** Everett Ferguson (Abilene Christian University, church-history specialist) wrote this 2003 (3rd ed.) Eerdmans volume covering Hellenistic and Roman political history, philosophy (Stoicism, Epicureanism, Middle Platonism), religion (mystery cults, emperor cult), and Judaism in roughly 600 pages — widely assigned in graduate NT programs.
**Use in the path:** Read relevant chapters as needed (e.g. "Roman government" before Romans/Acts trial scenes, "Mystery religions" before 1 Corinthians) rather than cover-to-cover; treat as the reference to consult when [[roman-imperial-context]] or a Greco-Roman-philosophy question comes up in the Greek text.
**Strengths:** Comprehensive, well-organized by topic, extensive further-reading bibliographies per chapter, non-polemical descriptive tone.
**Weaknesses / cautions:** Dense reference-style prose, not built for cover-to-cover reading; 2003 edition slightly dated on some archaeology (e.g. more recent Ephesus/Corinth excavation results).
**Claims to note:** Emperor cult as a live social pressure on Christian communities in Asia Minor by the time of Revelation — **majority view** among historians of the period.
