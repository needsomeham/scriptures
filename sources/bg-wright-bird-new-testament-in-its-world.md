**In one line:** An evangelical-critical intro built around reconstructing the "storied worldview" a first-century reader brought to the text.
**What it is:** N.T. Wright (Oxford/St Andrews, historian of Second Temple Judaism and Paul) and Michael Bird (Ridley College, Melbourne) co-wrote this 2019 Zondervan Academic volume — history, literature, and theology in one large textbook, self-consciously answering Ehrman's from a confessing-but-critical angle.
**Use in the path:** Pair with [[bg-ehrman-new-testament-historical-introduction]] chapter-by-chapter to see where secular-critical and evangelical-critical readings converge vs. diverge — this is the deliberate divergence-rule pairing for the intro-textbook layer.
**Strengths:** Unusually strong on Second Temple Jewish context and Roman imperial setting (Wright's specialty); readable; workbook edition has study questions.
**Weaknesses / cautions:** Wright's "fresh perspective" reading of Paul is itself a **contested** position within Pauline scholarship (dissent: traditional Reformed readings, and some New Perspective scholars like Sanders/Dunn diverge from Wright's specifics too) — treat Wright's synthesis as one voice, not the finish line.
**Claims to note:** Gospels as ancient biography (bios) — **majority view**, shared with Ehrman. Paul's "fresh perspective" on justification — **contested**.
