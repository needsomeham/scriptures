**In one line:** The complete digitized Codex Sinaiticus (4th c., one of the two oldest complete NT manuscripts), free online with transcription and translation.
**What it is:** A collaborative project (British Library, Leipzig University Library, National Library of Russia, Saint Catherine's Monastery/Saint Catherine Foundation) that reunited and digitized the dispersed leaves of Codex Sinaiticus (Aleph/01), publishing full page images, diplomatic transcription, and English translation at codexsinaiticus.org [T-09].
**Use in the path:** Beginner-friendly single-manuscript deep dive — pick a familiar passage and read it directly off a 4th-century page image with transcription support, a concrete anchor before abstract discussion of text-types.
**Strengths:** Free, complete manuscript (not fragments), excellent image quality, scholarly transcription with corrector layers marked (Sinaiticus has multiple scribal correction hands, itself a case study in transmission).
**Weaknesses / cautions:** One manuscript only — don't generalize "what Sinaiticus reads" to "what the earliest text read"; Sinaiticus is Alexandrian text-type and its readings are weighed, not simply adopted, by NA/UBS editors.
**Claims to note:** n/a (primary artifact).
