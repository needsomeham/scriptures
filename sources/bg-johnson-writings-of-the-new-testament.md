**In one line:** A Catholic scholar's literary-and-theological intro — useful counterweight when Ehrman's history-only frame feels reductive.
**What it is:** Luke Timothy Johnson (Emory University, former Benedictine monk, PhD Yale) wrote this 2010 (3rd ed.) Fortress Press volume. Unlike Ehrman or Brown, Johnson foregrounds each writing's literary artistry and theological argument as historical data in its own right, not just external facts about author/date.
**Use in the path:** Read Johnson's chapter on a book *after* the historical-background chapters (Ehrman/Brown) to see how a text's rhetoric and theology cohere as a first-century composition — useful once you're already tracking a book's Greek structure.
**Strengths:** Sophisticated close reading; takes ancient rhetorical conventions seriously; often cited as a moderate-to-critical Catholic counterpoint to both Ehrman and conservative evangelical intros.
**Weaknesses / cautions:** Assumes some prior familiarity with historical-critical vocabulary; less useful as a first exposure to a book than Powell or Ehrman.
**Claims to note:** Johnson has argued (in other work) against some of the more skeptical dating consensus for Acts and the Pastorals — a **minority view** relative to Ehrman/Brown's majority dating, worth naming per the divergence rule.
