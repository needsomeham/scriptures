**In one line:** The Catholic historical-critical benchmark — dense, exhaustive per-book scholarly-debate summaries.
**What it is:** Raymond E. Brown (1928–1998), Sulpician priest and Union Theological Seminary professor, was among the most respected Catholic biblical scholars of the 20th century. This 1997 Anchor Bible Reference volume (also in an abridged student edition by Marion Soards) surveys authorship, date, structure, and major interpretive questions for every NT book with unusual evenhandedness.
**Use in the path:** Use as a reference to consult per book rather than read cover-to-cover — go to its chapter on a given book when [[bg-ehrman-new-testament-historical-introduction]] or [[bg-wright-bird-new-testament-in-its-world]] disagree, since Brown typically lays out both sides before stating his own (moderate-critical) view.
**Strengths:** Extremely balanced presentation of scholarly options; imprimatur-approved yet fully historical-critical (accepts, e.g., non-Pauline authorship for some epistles) — a useful "middle" data point in the divergence rule.
**Weaknesses / cautions:** Dense reference prose, not built for linear reading; 1997 publication means post-2000 papyrological/DSS scholarship is absent.
**Claims to note:** Johannine community hypothesis (multi-stage composition of John) — presented as **majority view** among critical scholars, with conservative dissent (e.g. Carson, D.A.) noted.
