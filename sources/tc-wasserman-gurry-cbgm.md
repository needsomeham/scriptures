**In one line:** The best available plain-language introduction to the CBGM's graph-theoretic method — likely the single most appealing text-critical resource for this learner's technical background.
**What it is:** Tommy Wasserman (Ansgar University College, Norway) and Peter J. Gurry (Phoenix Seminary; also a leading ETC-blog contributor) wrote this 2017 SBL Press volume (Resources for Biblical Study 80) specifically to make CBGM accessible outside the small circle of INTF specialists, using worked examples. Confirmed not open-access as of 2026 — purchase or library access required; SBL sells both print and e-book [T-13].
**Use in the path:** Read once you understand basic textual criticism (after [[tc-metzger-ehrman-text-of-the-new-testament]]) and want the technical mechanics of [[tc-cbgm]] — frame it explicitly as "a coherence graph over witnesses, with local stemmata per variation unit and iterative refinement toward pre-genealogical coherence," which will map naturally onto graph-algorithm intuition.
**Strengths:** Written for a general scholarly (non-specialist) audience by two respected younger-generation TC scholars; includes worked examples and directly addresses common misunderstandings/objections to CBGM.
**Weaknesses / cautions:** Assumes prior TC vocabulary (text-types, apparatus); CBGM itself remains actively debated regarding how much it should override traditional external-evidence judgment — see cautions in [[tc-cbgm]].
**Claims to note:** Presents CBGM as now `majority view`-adjacent among INTF-aligned scholars for editing the ECM, while noting `contested` questions about how CBGM output should be weighed against traditional genealogical/text-type reasoning (e.g., criticisms voiced by some Byzantine-priority and thoroughgoing-eclectic scholars).
