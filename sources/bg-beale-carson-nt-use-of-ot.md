**In one line:** The essential reference for every OT quotation and allusion in the NT — indispensable once you notice a NT writer is quoting something.
**What it is:** G.K. Beale (Westminster Theological Seminary) and D.A. Carson (Trinity Evangelical Divinity School) edited this 2007 Baker Academic/Apollos volume (1,239 pp.), with specialist contributors treating every NT book's OT citations/allusions in turn — textual form used (MT vs LXX), original OT context, and how the NT author redeploys it.
**Use in the path:** Consult the relevant book's chapter whenever you hit a clear OT quotation while reading the Greek NT (e.g. Matthew's fulfillment citations, Paul's catena in Romans 3, Hebrews' use of Psalms) — this is the primary tool for [[septuagint-and-the-nt]] in practice.
**Strengths:** Comprehensive, scholarly, explicit about whether a NT author is following LXX or a Hebrew-based reading, and about the hermeneutical move being made (typology, direct fulfillment, analogy).
**Weaknesses / cautions:** Contributors vary in how confident vs. speculative their claims about authorial intent are — the volume is generally careful to flag this, but a beginner should still watch for claims that outrun the evidence (e.g. asserting a specific typological "intent" the NT author never states).
**Claims to note:** Matthew's formula quotations sometimes follow neither pure MT nor pure LXX but an independent or mixed textual tradition — **majority view** among text-critical specialists.
