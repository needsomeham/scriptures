**In one line:** The field-standard NT Greek lexicon — the first reference to consult for a word's full attested range of meaning in the NT and contemporary literature.
**What it is:** Originating with Walter Bauer (German, "Bauer's Wörterbuch"), translated/expanded by William Arndt, F. Wilbur Gingrich, and Frederick Danker (3rd ed., "BDAG," University of Chicago Press, 2000). Cites primary-source evidence (papyri, inscriptions, contemporary Greek authors) for every sense listed, not just NT usage.
**Use in the path:** Stage 4 (Reference/Advanced), used from Stage 3 onward for specific hard words — this is the lexicon to reach for once a word's meaning genuinely matters for interpretation, not for routine vocabulary building (use context and a reader's edition glossary for that). Danker also produced a cheaper, single-volume **Concise Greek-English Lexicon of the New Testament** (Concise BDAG, Univ. of Chicago Press) that trims the primary-source citations for a lower-cost, still-authoritative option when full BDAG's price is prohibitive.
**Strengths:** Unmatched depth and citation of primary evidence; the shared reference point of nearly all NT scholarship ("per BDAG...").
**Weaknesses / cautions:** Expensive; dense reference-work prose, not built for casual browsing; entries assume familiarity with abbreviations and lexicographic conventions.
**Claims to note:** none load-bearing beyond its own entries; treat each BDAG sense as evidence-backed but still an editorial judgment about how to divide a word's range of meaning.
