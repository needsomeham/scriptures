**In one line:** A first-year grammar built around continuous NT reading passages from the start, with an aspect-first (not tense-first) account of the verb.
**What it is:** Rodney J. Decker (PhD, late professor of NT at Baptist Bible Seminary) designed this text explicitly to front-load reading: each chapter works through real, only lightly adapted NT text rather than isolated drill sentences, and integrates verbal-aspect theory (following Porter/Fanning-informed scholarship) from the outset.
**Use in the path:** Stage 2 (Core Grammar) — the best-fit grammar for this learner's stated reading-first preference; consider it instead of or alongside [[mounce-basics-of-biblical-greek]] specifically because it reaches connected text faster and treats aspect (not just tense) as central, which a technical/systematic thinker will find conceptually cleaner.
**Strengths:** Reading-first structure, up-to-date aspect theory, free instructor eSources from Baker Academic.
**Weaknesses / cautions:** Smaller adoption than Mounce means fewer third-party supplements (flashcard decks, video walkthroughs); Decker's death (2014) means the text is not being revised further.
**Claims to note:** Verbal aspect (not Aktionsart/tense-prominence) as the primary category for the Greek verb is a **majority view** in current NT Greek linguistics, though tense-prominent treatments (as in older Wallace-style grammars) remain widely taught — **contested** in degree, not in the existence of aspect itself.
