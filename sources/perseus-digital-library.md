**In one line:** Tufts University's open digital library of ancient Greek (and Latin) texts with click-to-parse morphological analysis for nearly every word.
**What it is:** Maintained by Tufts University (founding editor Gregory Crane), Perseus hosts a huge corpus of classical and some Koine-adjacent texts with an interactive reading environment: click any word to see its parsing and a lexicon entry (LSJ).
**Use in the path:** Stage 3–4 — useful once the learner wants to read Koine-adjacent or Second Temple Greek texts (Septuagint excerpts, Josephus, Philo) beyond the NT itself, or wants to see a word parsed in a wider non-NT corpus for contextual range. Its own NT text holdings are thin; pair with a reader's edition for the NT proper.
**Strengths:** Free, huge corpus, genuine scholarly pedigree, morphological parser is transparent (shows its reasoning).
**Weaknesses / cautions:** Interface feels dated; parser occasionally offers multiple ambiguous options requiring judgment; not NT-focused, so best as a wide-reading extension rather than the daily reading tool.
**Claims to note:** none load-bearing.
