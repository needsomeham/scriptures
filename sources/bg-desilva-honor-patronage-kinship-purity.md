**In one line:** The most accessible entry point to the "cultural values" reading of the NT (honor-shame, patronage, kinship, purity) with direct payoff for specific passages.
**What it is:** David A. deSilva (Ashland Theological Seminary, PhD Emory) wrote this 2000 IVP Academic volume, popularizing (in more measured form) the social-science approach associated with the Context Group (Malina, Rohrbaugh, Neyrey) for a broader seminary/lay-scholar audience.
**Use in the path:** Read once, early, as orientation to [[honor-shame-culture]] before it recurs across many NT passages (patron-client language in Paul's letters, purity controversies in the Gospels, kinship-language for the church).
**Strengths:** Clear, well-organized by value-domain, generous with concrete NT examples per chapter.
**Weaknesses / cautions:** Presents the four values as more uniformly Mediterranean-wide than the primary evidence always supports; treat the model as a heuristic lens, not a fixed grid — see caution in [[honor-shame-culture]] and [[bg-malina-rohrbaugh-social-science-commentary]].
**Claims to note:** Honor-shame as a "pivotal value" across the Mediterranean including Judea — **majority view** in social-science criticism, but **contested** by scholars who argue the model over-generalizes across distinct sub-cultures and time periods (e.g., critiques summarized in Esler and others).
