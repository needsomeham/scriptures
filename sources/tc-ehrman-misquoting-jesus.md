**In one line:** The best-known popular book on NT textual criticism — historically accurate on the underlying facts, but its framing ("changed the Bible") is widely contested by scholars, including some who agree with Ehrman on the textual details.
**What it is:** Bart D. Ehrman (UNC-Chapel Hill; also co-author of the T1 textbook [[tc-metzger-ehrman-text-of-the-new-testament]]) wrote this 2005 popular trade book (HarperSanFrancisco) presenting NT textual variation to a general audience, framed around his own move away from evangelical inerrancy.
**Use in the path:** Optional, beginner-stage orientation only — read as motivation/context, not as a technical source; immediately pair with [[tc-hixson-gurry-myths-and-mistakes]] for the corrective side, and prefer this vault's own [[tc-major-disputed-passages]] concept note (built from UBS/Metzger primary sources) for the actual textual facts.
**Strengths:** Engaging, accurate on the base facts about variant counts and named disputed passages (Mark 16, Comma Johanneum, John 7:53-8:11); a real on-ramp to the field for a lay reader.
**Weaknesses / cautions:** Widely criticized — including by scholars who share Ehrman's TC methodology — for rhetorical framing that implies the *scale* of variation threatens confident reconstruction, when the same textual facts (in his own T1 textbook) are presented as manageable by reasoned eclecticism; treat its narrative arc as `speculative`/`contested`, its variant catalog as generally accurate.
**Claims to note:** Ehrman's headline claim that "we don't even know what the authors originally wrote" for many passages is `contested` — most TC scholars (including moderate critics of Ehrman) hold that the vast majority of the NT text is reconstructible with high confidence, with a bounded set of genuinely uncertain passages (see [[tc-major-disputed-passages]]).
