**In one line:** The standard intermediate/advanced NT Greek syntax reference — an exegetical grammar organized by grammatical category with copious NT examples.
**What it is:** Daniel B. Wallace (PhD Dallas Theological Seminary; founder of the Center for the Study of New Testament Manuscripts) wrote this as the successor to Dana & Mantey, cataloguing syntactic categories (genitive uses, participle types, conditional sentences, etc.) each illustrated from real NT verses with exegetical discussion.
**Use in the path:** Stage 4 (Reference/Advanced) — not a first read cover-to-cover; consult it the way one consults a reference grammar, when a specific construction met in reading (e.g., "why is this genitive translated as 'of'?") needs a systematic answer. Its example-driven format actually supports reading-first study well precisely because each entry starts from real text.
**Strengths:** Comprehensive, example-dense, widely cited so it's the shared reference vocabulary of the field ("Wallace category X").
**Weaknesses / cautions:** Wallace's own theological/exegetical judgment calls (e.g., some disputed genitive/participle classifications) reflect a broadly evangelical, tense-prominent (pre-aspect-theory) grammatical tradition; cross-check contested classification calls against Decker or Runge, who write from an aspect-prominent and discourse-linguistic standpoint respectively.
**Claims to note:** Individual syntactic category assignments in disputed verses (e.g., Granville Sharp construction debates, "faith of/in Christ" genitive) are **contested**; Wallace argues one side and names the debate but is not a neutral tabulation — check [[moc-nt-background]] bridges for specific disputed passages.
