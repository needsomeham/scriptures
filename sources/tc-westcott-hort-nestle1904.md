**In one line:** The two historically pivotal Alexandrian-leaning editions that founded modern textual criticism and underlie NA/UBS's lineage — both fully public domain.
**What it is:** B. F. Westcott and F. J. A. Hort's *The New Testament in the Original Greek* (1881, Cambridge) introduced systematic weighting toward the Alexandrian text-type (via Codex Vaticanus/Sinaiticus) and genealogical reasoning that shaped every later critical edition. Eberhard Nestle's 1904 GNT (British and Foreign Bible Society) was the direct ancestor of the Nestle-Aland series. Both are on archive.org and sites.google.com/site/nestle1904 in full text [T-06].
**Use in the path:** Read after NA28/apparatus literacy, as history-of-scholarship background for [[tc-text-types-and-manuscript-families]] — useful to see how little the base text has moved since 1881 versus how much apparatus/method has advanced.
**Strengths:** Free, historically foundational, shows the reasoning (preface essays) behind privileging early Alexandrian witnesses — the direct ancestor of the modern `majority view`.
**Weaknesses / cautions:** Over a century of new papyri (P66, P75, etc.) and manuscript discoveries since 1881/1904 mean neither is a current critical text — read as history, not as your working text.
**Claims to note:** Westcott-Hort's preference for Vaticanus/Sinaiticus as the "neutral text" is now itself `contested` as a category (modern eclecticism doesn't rank one manuscript as neutral) — see [[tc-reasoned-eclecticism]].
