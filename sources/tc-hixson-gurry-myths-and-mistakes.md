**In one line:** A chapter-by-chapter correction of popular overstatements on both the "Bible is corrupted" and "Bible is perfectly preserved" sides — good calibration reading.
**What it is:** Edited by Elijah Hixson and Peter J. Gurry (both active Evangelical Textual Criticism bloggers/scholars), with chapters by multiple TC specialists (IVP Academic, 2019). Each chapter targets one widely-repeated claim (e.g., "we don't have the originals so we can't know the text," "the number of variants is huge and scary," "a manuscript's date is highly certain") and corrects it against current scholarship [T-14].
**Use in the path:** Read early-to-mid in TC study, right after or alongside [[tc-metzger-ehrman-text-of-the-new-testament]], specifically to inoculate against both popular-apologetics overclaiming and popular-skeptic overclaiming (including some claims in [[tc-ehrman-misquoting-jesus]]) before you've built the apparatus literacy to check primary sources yourself.
**Strengths:** Explicitly corrects claims from *both* directions (evangelical apologetics and Ehrman-style popularizations); each chapter is written by a credentialed contributor with citations, not just assertion.
**Weaknesses / cautions:** Edited/authored by scholars broadly sympathetic to reasoned eclecticism and evangelical institutions (Hixson: Museum of the Bible/Cambridge-trained; Gurry: Phoenix Seminary) — read as `majority view`-aligned corrective, not a neutral outside referee; still useful precisely because it cites primary scholarship for each correction.
**Claims to note:** Individual chapters make targeted `consensus`-level corrections (e.g., manuscript dating uncertainty ranges) — treat each chapter's specific claim on its own merits rather than the book as one monolithic position.
