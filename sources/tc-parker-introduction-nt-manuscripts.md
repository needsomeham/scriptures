**In one line:** A more advanced, manuscript-centered alternative/supplement to Metzger & Ehrman, from a scholar who argues the NT text should be understood as a living, plural tradition rather than a single fixed original.
**What it is:** David C. Parker (University of Birmingham, longtime ECM/INTF collaborator and IGNTP director) wrote this 2008 Cambridge University Press survey, organizing by manuscript categories (papyri, majuscules, minuscules, versions, patristic citations) before addressing method and history [T-12].
**Use in the path:** Read after [[tc-metzger-ehrman-text-of-the-new-testament]], once you want a second, more technical treatment and exposure to Parker's own influential (and `contested`) "living text" view — that textual plurality is a feature of early Christian textual culture, not simply corruption to be resolved to one original.
**Strengths:** Deep manuscript-category detail useful for someone who wants primary-source fluency; written by a scholar directly inside the ECM/CBGM project, so it connects well to [[tc-cbgm]] and [[tc-ntvmr-intf-ecm]].
**Weaknesses / cautions:** Denser and more assumes-the-field than Metzger & Ehrman; Parker's "living text" framing is a `minority view`/distinctive position within TC, not the field consensus — most editors (NA/UBS/ECM committees) still aim to reconstruct an initial text, even while using CBGM's plural-witness data.
**Claims to note:** Parker's thesis that early textual plurality was normal, not merely erroneous, is `contested`/`minority view` — most mainstream TC (e.g., Metzger & Ehrman, ECM editors) still frames variation as transmission history to be reconstructed toward an initial text.
