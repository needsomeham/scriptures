**In one line:** A frequency-and-cognate-organized vocabulary reference — useful as a supporting index, not a spine, for a reading-first learner.
**What it is:** Bruce M. Metzger (PhD Princeton; chair of the UBS Greek NT editorial committee and the Nestle-Aland committee — see [[moc-textual-criticism]]) compiled NT words grouped by frequency and etymological family, with brief glosses, to help students consolidate vocabulary already met in reading.
**Use in the path:** Supporting reference only, used *after* meeting a word in actual reading (1 John, Mark, or wherever the reader's edition footnotes stopped covering it) — the learner should look a word up here to see its frequency and word-family, not memorize the list cover-to-cover, per this vault's context-over-lists principle.
**Strengths:** Organizing by cognate family builds real pattern recognition (e.g., seeing λόγος, λέγω, λογίζομαι together) rather than arbitrary alphabetical lists; short, cheap, easy to dip into.
**Weaknesses / cautions:** A frequency list by itself teaches no context — pair every session with actual reading, never use as a standalone memorization drill.
**Claims to note:** none load-bearing.
