**In one line:** Free translation of the major Hellenistic-Jewish philosopher contemporary with Jesus — the best window into Diaspora Jewish thought that shaped NT vocabulary (esp. Logos, allegory).
**What it is:** Philo of Alexandria (c. 20 BCE–50 CE) was a Hellenized Jewish philosopher-exegete who read the LXX allegorically through a Platonic/Stoic lens. C.D. Yonge's 19th-century English translation is public domain and hosted free at earlychristianwritings.com/yonge/ (also Internet Archive, Logos).
**Use in the path:** Sample *On the Creation* or *On the Life of Moses* when studying John's Logos prologue or Hebrews' Platonic-sounding language — Philo shows how an educated first-century Jew outside Palestine already blended biblical and Greek philosophical categories.
**Strengths:** Free, complete; directly illuminates vocabulary (Logos, Sophia) NT writers share with a wider Hellenistic-Jewish intellectual world.
**Weaknesses / cautions:** Yonge's translation is dated (Victorian-era) prose; Philo is not "the background" of any single NT author (no direct literary dependence is established) but rather a contemporary data point for what Hellenistic-Jewish thought could sound like — don't overstate direct influence.
**Claims to note:** Whether John's Logos concept draws directly on Philo — **speculative**/**contested**; most scholars see a shared Hellenistic-Jewish milieu rather than direct literary dependence.
