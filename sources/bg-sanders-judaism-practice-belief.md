**In one line:** The landmark academic reconstruction of ordinary "common Judaism" in the period the NT was written — essential once you want more than a textbook paragraph.
**What it is:** E.P. Sanders (1937–2022; Duke, Oxford, McMaster) wrote this 1992 monograph (Trinity Press International/SCM), synthesizing archaeology, rabbinic sources, Josephus, and the DSS to describe how most Jews of the period actually practiced their religion — distinct from his earlier, more famous *Paul and Palestinian Judaism* (1977), which launched the "New Perspective on Paul."
**Use in the path:** Read after a general intro (e.g. Powell/Ehrman), before tackling [[second-temple-judaism]] in depth or reading Paul's letters closely — Sanders is the source most other background books lean on for "what did Second Temple Judaism actually look like."
**Strengths:** Exhaustive primary-source grounding; corrected older Christian-scholarship caricatures of Judaism as legalistic "works-righteousness."
**Weaknesses / cautions:** Sanders's "covenantal nomism" model is highly influential but **contested** — dissent includes traditional Lutheran/Reformed readings of Paul (e.g., Stephen Westerholm) and some who argue Sanders understates diversity within Second Temple Judaism (e.g., the multi-volume *Justification and Variegated Nomism* project, ed. Carson/O'Brien/Seifrid).
**Claims to note:** "Covenantal nomism" as the dominant pattern of Jewish religion in this period — **majority view**, not **consensus**.
