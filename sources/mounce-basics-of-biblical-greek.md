**In one line:** The most widely used first-year seminary Greek grammar, systematic and vocabulary-graded.
**What it is:** William D. Mounce (PhD Aberdeen; former Greek editor, ESV) wrote this grammar+workbook pair, now in its 4th edition (Zondervan). Organizes Greek by paradigm (noun cases, then verb system) rather than by reading connected text.
**Use in the path:** Stage 2 (Core Grammar, weeks 9–24) — spine for systematic morphology once the alphabet and basic reading habit (Stage 1, via [[daily-dose-of-greek]] and a reader's edition) are in place. Pairs each chapter with graded exercises; supplement with real NT sentences early rather than waiting for the book's own reading passages, which arrive late.
**Strengths:** Extremely well scaffolded, huge ancillary ecosystem (videos, flashcards, professor's site), forgiving pacing.
**Weaknesses / cautions:** Paradigm-first design front-loads memorization before contextual reading; verbal aspect treatment is dated relative to Decker/Porter/Campbell's aspect-focused work. Vocabulary lists are decontextualized — resist rote-memorizing them in isolation.
**Claims to note:** none beyond standard grammar description; not a site of contested scholarship.
