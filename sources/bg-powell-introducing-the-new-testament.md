**In one line:** A mainline-Protestant intro emphasizing literary/narrative reading alongside historical background, with a genuinely free companion site.
**What it is:** Mark Allan Powell (Trinity Lutheran Seminary) wrote this 2018 (2nd ed.) Baker Academic textbook. Distinctive for balancing historical-critical content with narrative criticism (how the text works as story) — a middle path between Ehrman's history-only approach and purely theological introductions.
**Use in the path:** Good first-read intro before specializing; the free "eSources" (bakeracademic.com) give chapter summaries, maps, and timelines useful for quick review between deeper background reading.
**Strengths:** Approachable for a beginner, evenly ecumenical in tone, strong maps/timelines, free companion materials lower the cost of ownership.
**Weaknesses / cautions:** Less depth than Brown or Ferguson on contested historical questions; narrative-critical framing can understate how much scholarly disagreement underlies a given "story" reading.
**Claims to note:** Narrative-critical method treats implied author/reader as literary constructs distinct from historical author/audience — standard method, not itself a contested historical claim.
