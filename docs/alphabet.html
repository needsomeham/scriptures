Diacritics
recognise them; you need not reproduce them yet
᾿ ῾Breathings
Every word starting with a vowel or ρ carries a smooth breathing (silent) or a rough breathing (adds /h/) over the first vowel — or the second, in a diphthong.
´ ` ῀Accents
Acute, grave, circumflex. They mark an ancient pitch change, not stress. Learn to recognise them, not to reproduce them: you can read and parse correctly without them.
ᾳ ῃ ῳIota subscript
A small ι written under α, η or ω — a former diphthong, now silent. It still matters for parsing: it is part of some dative endings.
¨Diaeresis
Over ι or υ, marking that it starts a new syllable rather than forming a diphthong with the vowel before it (Ἠσαΐας).
· ;Punctuation
A raised dot is the semicolon/colon; a semicolon is the question mark — the opposite of English. Comma and period look the same as in English.
