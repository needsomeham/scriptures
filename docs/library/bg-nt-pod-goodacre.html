NT Pod (Mark Goodacre)

Source
SourceT2bbeginnerfreeNT background#free, #podcast, #historical-critical

In one line: Short, focused podcast episodes by a named NT scholar on one question at a time — good low-effort supplement.

What it is: Mark Goodacre (Duke University, PhD Oxford, specialist in the Synoptic Problem and Thomas) has produced NT Pod episodes (roughly 10–20 minutes each) since 2007, each tackling one discrete critical question (e.g. "Did Mark know Q?", "Was Paul in prison in Ephesus?").

Use in the path: Use as a commute/walk supplement — pick episodes matching whatever book you're currently reading in learning-path; not a substitute for a structured course but good for reinforcing specific debates.

Strengths: Free, short, named academic credentials, topically searchable via the blog archive (podacre.blogspot.com).

Weaknesses / cautions: Goodacre is a noted proponent of the Farrer hypothesis (Luke used Matthew directly, no Q) — a minority view relative to the Two-Source hypothesis majority view; he is candid about this but a beginner should know the episode reflects one side of a live debate.

Claims to note: Q document's existence — majority view (Two-Source), Goodacre's Farrer hypothesis is the leading minority view dissent.
