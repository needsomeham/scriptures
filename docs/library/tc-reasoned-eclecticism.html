Reasoned eclecticism (and thoroughgoing eclecticism)

Concept
ConceptTextual criticism#method

In one line: The dominant modern method for choosing the "best" reading at each variant point weighs both external evidence (which/how many/how old witnesses) and internal evidence (which reading better explains the others arising) — no single manuscript or family is followed automatically.

What it is: Reasoned eclecticism is the method behind NA28/UBS5 [T-01][T-02]: at each variation unit, editors weigh external evidence (date, geographic spread, text-type distribution of witnesses) against internal evidence (which reading a scribe was more likely to create — the harder/shorter/less-harmonized reading is usually preferred, per lectio difficilior and lectio brevior principles). A more radical sibling method, thoroughgoing (or "rigorous") eclecticism, associated with J. K. Elliott (1943-2024, University of Leeds), gives internal/transcriptional evidence near-total priority and treats manuscript pedigree as largely irrelevant — Elliott argued external evidence is too unreliable to weight consistently [T-19].

Why it matters for reading the NT: This is the reasoning you'll actually be doing when you read a Metzger Textual Commentary entry — every UBS letter rating is the output of exactly this internal/external weighing process, made explicit.

State of scholarship: Reasoned eclecticism is the majority view/near-consensus method among academic textual critics and is what NA/UBS/ECM committees practice. Thoroughgoing eclecticism (Elliott) is a minority view taken seriously in the literature but adopted by few working editions. Byzantine priority (see tc-byzantine-priority-debate) rejects both in favor of manuscript-count-weighted external evidence.

Where it breaks / cautions: "Reasoned" doesn't mean algorithmic or free of judgment calls — different reasoned-eclectic editors (compare NA28 vs. THGNT, tc-tyndale-house-greek-new-testament) reach different verdicts in hundreds of places from the same method, because internal-evidence judgments are inherently interpretive.
