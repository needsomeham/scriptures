A Manual Greek Lexicon of the New Testament (Abbott-Smith)

Source
SourceT2intermediatefree (public domain, archive.org)Greek learning#lexicon, #public-domain, #free

In one line: A compact, free, public-domain NT lexicon — a workable BDAG substitute for a learner not ready to buy the full reference.

What it is: George Abbott-Smith (Canadian classicist/biblical scholar) published this manual lexicon in 1922, based on Grimm-Thayer and contemporary LXX/papyrological scholarship of its era; it fell into public domain and is now digitized (archive.org PDF; also packaged into several free Bible-software modules).

Use in the path: Stage 2–3 — a free everyday lexicon while reading, before investing in BDAG; entries are short enough to check quickly without derailing a reading session, closer to a reader's-edition glossary in depth but covering the whole NT vocabulary.

Strengths: Free, public domain, still linguistically respectable for basic sense ranges, portable digital formats.

Weaknesses / cautions: Over a century old — misses post-1922 papyrological and lexical-semantic advances (semantic-domain theory, updated Koine papyri evidence) that BDAG and Louw-Nida incorporate; do not use for load-bearing exegetical claims without cross-checking BDAG.

Claims to note: none load-bearing; superseded reference for close scholarly work.
