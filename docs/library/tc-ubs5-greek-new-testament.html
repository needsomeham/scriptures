The Greek New Testament, 5th ed. (UBS5)

Source
SourceT1referencepaid (~$35-60 print; bundled in Bible software)Textual criticism#critical-edition, #greek-text

In one line: The same critical text as NA28, formatted for translators with a simplified apparatus and letter-grade confidence ratings (A-D).

What it is: Published by the United Bible Societies (German Bible Society/Deutsche Bibelgesellschaft), edited by the same INTF-linked committee (Barbara Aland, Metzger et al.); 5th edition (2014) matches NA28's text exactly. Its apparatus is pared to variants that matter for translation, each rated {A}(virtually certain) to {D}(great difficulty deciding) [T-02].

Use in the path: Use alongside NA28 once reading full apparatus entries — UBS5's letter grades are the fastest way to gauge how settled a given reading is, referenced throughout tc-major-disputed-passages.

Strengths: The {A}-{D} rating is an explicit, quantifiable confidence signal — appeals directly to a probabilistic-reasoning mindset; smaller apparatus is less overwhelming for a learner than NA28's.

Weaknesses / cautions: Fewer witnesses cited than NA28 (translation-focused, not exhaustive); ratings reflect committee judgment, not a formal statistical method — don't over-read the letter as a probability.

Claims to note: Same underlying text as NA28 — a consensus among mainstream (non-Byzantine-priority) textual critics that NA/UBS represents the best current reasoned-eclectic reconstruction; Byzantine-priority and KJV-only positions dissent (see tc-byzantine-priority-debate).
