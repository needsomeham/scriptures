Greek-English Lexicon of the New Testament Based on Semantic Domains (Louw-Nida)

Source
SourceT1advancedpaid (~$70–100 for the 2-vol set; also in Bible software packages)Greek learning#lexicon, #semantic-domains, #reference

In one line: A lexicon organized by meaning-group ("semantic domain") rather than alphabetically, showing which NT words compete or overlap in a given conceptual space.

What it is: Johannes P. Louw and Eugene A. Nida (Nida a pioneer of modern translation theory, United Bible Societies) grouped the entire NT vocabulary into ~93 semantic domains (e.g., "Kinship Terms," "Hostility/Strife"), so a reader can see every word available for a concept side by side, with nuanced distinctions among near-synonyms.

Use in the path: Stage 4 (Reference/Advanced) — exactly the "understand in context" tool this learner wants over flashcards: instead of memorizing a word in isolation, look up its domain and see how NT authors distinguished it from near-synonyms (e.g., different Greek words for "love," "know," "authority").

Strengths: Directly supports contextual/semantic understanding rather than gloss-matching; widely used in translation and lexical-semantics scholarship.

Weaknesses / cautions: Two-volume format (domains + alphabetical index) is less convenient for a quick single-word lookup than BDAG; some domain-assignment judgment calls are the authors' own analysis, not uncontested fact.

Claims to note: Domain analysis reflects Nida's componential/semantic-field theory of meaning, a majority view approach in lexical semantics but not the only framework (cognitive-linguistic and prototype-based approaches offer alternative accounts of some domain boundaries).
