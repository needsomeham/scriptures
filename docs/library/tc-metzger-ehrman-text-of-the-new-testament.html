Metzger & Ehrman, "The Text of the New Testament" (4th ed.)

Source
SourceT1intermediatepaid (~$35-50; used copies cheaper)Textual criticism#textbook, #transmission-history

In one line: The standard graduate-level survey of NT textual criticism — history of transmission, manuscript families, and method — the field's default textbook.

What it is: Bruce M. Metzger (Princeton Theological Seminary, chief editor of UBS/NA critical texts for decades) wrote the first three editions; Bart D. Ehrman (UNC-Chapel Hill, PhD under Metzger) updated the 4th edition (Oxford University Press, 2005), adding material on scribal habits and social history of transmission [T-10].

Use in the path: Read cover-to-cover once comfortable with basic Greek reading (~month 6-9) as the backbone text for tc-text-types-and-manuscript-families and transmission history generally, before or alongside NA28 apparatus practice.

Strengths: Comprehensive, clearly organized (manuscripts → versions → history of criticism → method), written by the actual editor of the critical texts you're using; balanced presentation of methods including Byzantine priority as a named alternative.

Weaknesses / cautions: Ehrman's own popular writing (see tc-ehrman-misquoting-jesus) is more polemical than this textbook; the textbook itself stays measured, but know that Ehrman brings a documented skeptical-of-inerrancy stance to his broader public work — read his claims here on their textual-critical merits, which are widely respected even by scholars who reject his broader theological conclusions.

Claims to note: Presents reasoned eclecticism as the field's majority view while naming Byzantine priority and thoroughgoing eclecticism as live minority methods — a fair, non-advocacy survey per the textbook genre.
