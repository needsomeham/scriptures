Text-types and manuscript families

Concept
ConceptTextual criticism#manuscripts, #transmission

In one line: Manuscripts cluster into recognizable "family" patterns (Alexandrian, Byzantine, Western, Caesarean) based on shared readings, which is the raw data every text-critical method has to explain.

What it is: Across ~5,700+ known Greek NT manuscripts, scholars since Westcott-Hort (1881) have grouped witnesses by shared distinctive readings into rough families: Alexandrian (early, terse, e.g. Vaticanus B, Sinaiticus Aleph, P75) — generally favored by NA/UBS; Byzantine (majority of surviving manuscripts, but mostly later, 9th c.+, more fluent/harmonized text) — favored by tc-byzantine-priority-debate advocates; Western (distinctive, sometimes longer, e.g. Codex Bezae); and a smaller, less-agreed-upon Caesarean grouping. Family assignment is itself a simplification — the CBGM (tc-cbgm) was developed partly because manuscripts don't cleanly sort into strict families; contamination (mixed ancestry) is the norm, not the exception [T-06][T-10][T-12].

Why it matters for reading the NT: Knowing a variant's family distribution tells you whether it's an isolated scribal slip or a deep transmissional split — this is the raw material behind every apparatus entry and every UBS confidence rating.

State of scholarship: The existence of clustering is consensus; how much weight family membership should carry versus per-manuscript/per-reading (CBGM-style) analysis is contested — Parker (T1, tc-parker-introduction-nt-manuscripts) argues family categories are a useful but leaky heuristic, not a genealogical fact; traditional text-type-based reasoning still underlies most classroom teaching (e.g., Metzger & Ehrman, tc-metzger-ehrman-text-of-the-new-testament).

Where it breaks / cautions: Don't treat "Alexandrian = older = better" as a rule — age and text-type are correlated but not identical, and CBGM analysis has shown some "later" manuscripts preserve early readings via mixed transmission lines. Also: family boundaries were drawn from a small witness sample by Westcott-Hort; modern full-collation data (ECM) complicates the neat four-family picture.
