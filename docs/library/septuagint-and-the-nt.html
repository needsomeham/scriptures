The Septuagint and the New Testament

Concept
ConceptNT background#septuagint, #greek, #old-testament-in-new

In one line: The Septuagint (LXX), the pre-Christian Greek translation of the Hebrew scriptures, is the actual text NT authors usually quote, echo, and think in — not the Hebrew Masoretic Text behind most English Old Testaments.

What it is: Beginning with the Pentateuch (traditionally 3rd century BCE, Alexandria) and completed in stages through the 2nd–1st centuries BCE, the LXX translated the Hebrew Bible (plus some works never in the Hebrew canon, e.g. Wisdom of Solomon) into Koine Greek. By the first century CE it was the Bible most Diaspora Jews and virtually all Greek-speaking early Christians read.

Why it matters for reading the NT: A large share of NT "Old Testament quotations" match LXX wording, not the Hebrew — meaning the Greek vocabulary and theological connotations a NT author invokes are often LXX-specific (e.g. "glory" doxa carrying LXX kabod-Shekinah overtones absent from ordinary Greek usage; Isaiah 7:14's LXX parthenos "virgin" vs. the Hebrew almah "young woman," which underlies Matthew 1:23's fulfillment claim). Reading Greek without awareness of the LXX layer misses why an NT author chose a given word or read an OT text a certain way.

State of scholarship: That NT quotations of the OT track LXX wording more often than the Hebrew MT is consensus; the precise textual form used in any single citation (pure LXX, a Hebraizing revision, a testimonia/collection of proof-texts, or the author's own rendering) is often contested case by case and requires consulting a specialist source like Beale & Carson's commentary rather than assuming.

Where it breaks / cautions: The LXX was not a single fixed, uniform text in the first century — different books were translated by different translators with different styles, and some circulated in revised forms (proto-Theodotion, etc.) before Origen's later Hexapla. Don't assume "the LXX" is monolithic; a specific citation needs its specific textual history checked.
