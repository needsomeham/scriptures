Invitation to the Septuagint (Jobes & Silva)

Source
SourceT2intermediatepaid (~$40)NT background#septuagint, #textbook, #greek

In one line: The standard seminary-level textbook introduction to the Septuagint — origins, textual history, and its role behind the NT's Greek.

What it is: Karen H. Jobes (Wheaton College) and Moisés Silva (Westmont College, prominent NT Greek linguist) wrote this 2015 (2nd ed.) Baker Academic textbook, covering the LXX's translation history, textual criticism, and its citation by NT authors, with exercises for readers who know some Greek.

Use in the path: Read once you can handle basic Koine syntax, as a bridge between Greek-learning and septuagint-and-the-nt — it directly explains why NT quotations of the OT often don't match a modern English OT translation (which is usually from Hebrew).

Strengths: Written for readers with some Greek already, clear on why LXX matters for NT vocabulary and theology (e.g. how LXX word choices shaped terms like "glory" doxa, "righteousness" dikaiosynē).

Weaknesses / cautions: Assumes at least first-year Greek; the LXX's own textual history (multiple translators, revisions like Aquila/Symmachus/Theodotion) is itself a specialist field this book only introduces.

Claims to note: NT authors' OT quotations align with LXX wording more often than with the Hebrew Masoretic Text — consensus (though the ratio is debated in specific cases; see bg-beale-carson-nt-use-of-ot).
