The New Testament in the Original Greek: Byzantine Textform (Robinson-Pierpont)

Source
SourceT1intermediatefree (digital); print availableTextual criticism#critical-edition, #greek-text, #free, #byzantine-priority

In one line: The standard modern edition of the Byzantine/Majority text tradition, free online, representing the methodological alternative to NA/UBS reasoned eclecticism.

What it is: Compiled by Maurice A. Robinson and William G. Pierpont (2005, revised 2018) using a documented "Byzantine-priority" method that privileges manuscript-count and internal transmissional-probability arguments over the Alexandrian-leaning weighting of NA/UBS. Full text and morphology free via GitHub (biblicalhumanities.org, byztxt) and archive.org [T-05].

Use in the path: Read this only after you're comfortable with NA28/UBS5, specifically to understand tc-byzantine-priority-debate from a primary text rather than secondhand — compare a disputed verse (e.g., Mark 16:9-20, the Comma Johanneum) side-by-side with NA28.

Strengths: Free, well-documented rationale (Robinson's introduction lays out the method explicitly), lets you see what "majority of manuscripts" actually reads like verse-by-verse.

Weaknesses / cautions: Byzantine priority is a minority view among academic textual critics (most of the field favors reasoned eclecticism per tc-reasoned-eclecticism); conflated in popular discussion with KJV-onlyism, though Robinson himself is not KJV-only and argues on textual-transmission grounds, not doctrinal ones.

Claims to note: Robinson's core claim — that scribal transmission dynamics favor the majority reading surviving — is contested; mainstream reply is that manuscript count reflects later Byzantine-era copying volume, not textual priority (Metzger & Ehrman, tc-metzger-ehrman-text-of-the-new-testament).
