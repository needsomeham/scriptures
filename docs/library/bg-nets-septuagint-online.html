NETS — A New English Translation of the Septuagint (free online)

Source
SourceT2bintermediatefreeNT background#free, #primary-source, #septuagint

In one line: The full Septuagint in English, free online — read the actual text NT authors usually quote from.

What it is: Albert Pietersma and Benjamin G. Wright edited this scholarly LXX translation (Oxford UP, 2007); the complete electronic edition is hosted free by the University of Pennsylvania (ccat.sas.upenn.edu/nets/), one book at a time as searchable PDFs.

Use in the path: Whenever bg-beale-carson-nt-use-of-ot or a footnote flags a NT quotation as LXX-based, pull up the actual NETS text of that OT passage side-by-side with the NT Greek to see the wording match directly, rather than trusting a paraphrase.

Strengths: Free, complete, scholarly (NRSV-adjacent register), directly usable alongside Greek reading since it renders LXX wording rather than harmonizing to the Hebrew-based English OT you already know.

Weaknesses / cautions: NETS deliberately translates the LXX as a translation of a Hebrew Vorlage (an "interlinear paradigm"), which sometimes makes it read more literally/awkwardly than a translation aiming for natural English — that's a feature for comparison work, not a flaw to "fix."

Claims to note: N/A — primary text; claims belong to the analysis sources (bg-beale-carson-nt-use-of-ot, bg-jobes-silva-invitation-septuagint) that use it.
