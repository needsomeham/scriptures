The New Testament — A Historical Introduction (Ehrman)

Source
SourceT2beginnerpaid (~$80–100 new; used/rental much cheaper; earlier eds. on Internet Archive)NT background#introduction, #historical-critical, #textbook

In one line: The standard secular-critical NT introduction — historical method taught by demonstration on every book.

What it is: Bart Ehrman (Univ. of North Carolina, PhD Princeton, textual critic) wrote this widely-adopted undergraduate textbook; from the 8th edition Hugo Méndez co-authors. Now in its 8th/9th printings (Oxford UP). Treats each NT book as a historical artifact — authorship, date, audience, genre — bracketing theological claims.

Use in the path: Read the relevant chapter before or alongside each Gospel/epistle in learning-path; it is the fastest way to get "who wrote this, when, for whom, and what's disputed" without wading through journal literature first.

Strengths: Very clear prose, explicit about historical-critical method, generous with primary-source excerpts, strong on Synoptic Problem and pseudonymity debates.

Weaknesses / cautions: Ehrman's own positions (e.g. leaning toward pseudonymous authorship for several epistles) are presented as mainstream when some are actually majority view rather than consensus — cross-check against bg-brown-introduction-to-new-testament or bg-wright-bird-new-testament-in-its-world for the more traditional/evangelical counter-reading before treating a dating claim as settled.

Claims to note: Deutero-Pauline status of Ephesians/Colossians/Pastorals — majority view in critical scholarship, contested among evangelical scholars (dissent: Wright, Bird). Markan priority — consensus.
