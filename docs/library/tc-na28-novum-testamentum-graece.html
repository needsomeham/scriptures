Novum Testamentum Graece (NA28)

Source
SourceT1referencepaid (~$45-70 print; included in most Greek software)Textual criticism#critical-edition, #greek-text

In one line: The standard scholarly Greek NT text and apparatus used in nearly all seminaries and critical scholarship since 2012.

What it is: Produced by the Institute for New Testament Textual Research (INTF, Münster) under the Deutsche Bibelgesellschaft; the 28th edition (2012) updated the Catholic Epistles using early results of the Editio Critica Maior (ECM). Editors include Barbara Aland, Kurt Aland (d. 1994), Johannes Karavidopoulos, Carlo M. Martini, Bruce Metzger; current committee continues at INTF. A successor, NA29, is in preparation (no publication date announced as of 2026); it will fold in ECM results for Mark, Acts, and Revelation and reorder books to majority-manuscript sequence [T-01].

Use in the path: Terminal reference from ~month 6+ of Greek onward — read the running text alongside tc-metzger-textual-commentary-gnt to interpret the apparatus. Not a first reading text (footnotes assume apparatus literacy).

Strengths: Authoritative reasoned-eclectic text; apparatus documents variants against a controlled list of witnesses; basis for nearly all modern translations and for tc-cbgm research.

Weaknesses / cautions: Apparatus symbols require training (see tc-reading-a-critical-apparatus); not free (though NA27, textually near-identical outside Catholic Epistles, appears on some open archives); does not itself explain why a reading was chosen — that's Metzger's Commentary.

Claims to note: The text represents a majority view reasoned-eclectic reconstruction, not a consensus — Byzantine-priority scholars dispute its Alexandrian lean (see tc-byzantine-priority-debate).
