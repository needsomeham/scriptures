Reading a critical apparatus

Concept
ConceptTextual criticism#skill, #na28, #ubs5

In one line: The footnote block under an NA28/UBS5 page is a compact, symbol-coded record of every place manuscripts disagree on that page's text — learnable as a fixed notation system, not prose.

What it is: Each apparatus entry names a variation unit (the disputed word/phrase, marked in the text by a symbol like ° or ⸀), lists the readings proposed, and cites which witnesses support each — using sigla: papyri as 𝔓 + number (e.g. 𝔓⁴⁶), majuscules as bold capital letters or numbers (e.g. א/01 = Sinaiticus, B/03 = Vaticanus), minuscules as plain numbers, plus versional (Latin, Syriac, Coptic) and patristic citations, and family/group symbols (𝔐 = Majority text, 𝑓¹/𝑓¹³ = specific minuscule families). UBS5 additionally appends a bracketed letter grade {A}-{D} to its (smaller) set of flagged variants, reflecting committee confidence [T-01][T-02].

Why it matters for reading the NT: This is the literal skill this vault's mission targets — being able to open NA28 and understand, in real time, what is and isn't textually certain in the verse you're reading, rather than trusting a translator's footnote secondhand.

State of scholarship: n/a — this is notation, not a scholarly claim; but note the sigla and abbreviated Latin apparatus terms (e.g. sine add., pc, al) are consensus conventions maintained by INTF across editions.

Where it breaks / cautions: NA28 apparatus is negative and selective — absence of a variant does not always mean "no variant exists," only "not flagged as significant enough by these editors"; always pair a hard case with tc-metzger-textual-commentary-gnt rather than trying to reverse-engineer editorial reasoning from sigla alone. UBS5's apparatus is deliberately smaller than NA28's — a UBS "no entry" doesn't mean NA28 has none too.
